Moss Rose

Rosé Pine's syntax colors on a Vitesse canvas, in dark and light. The background, the text, the flat panels and the green accent come from Vitesse. Strings, functions, types and parameters keep their Rosé Pine hues, brought down in brightness so they sit a step below the text the way they do in Rosé Pine. Keywords take the green.

Languages and formats

Sixteen sample files, colored by role. These are rendered with a different highlighter than the editors use, so they show how the palette balances across languages, not the exact result in VS Code or Neovim. The files are in samples/ for opening in the real thing.

Palette

Ratios are WCAG contrast against the background. The same 21 values drive Ghostty, VS Code and Neovim. The strip under the terminal shows the 16 ANSI colors, normal above bright.

What came from where

DecisionSourceWhy
Pure black canvas, or whiteVitesse Black, Vitesse LightRosé Pine's bases are tinted purple and cream, which tints everything on them. A neutral canvas lets the muted accents carry the color.
Light accentsRosé Pine DawnDawn's hues, darkened. Its own values are too pale on white (its gold is 2.2:1). Each hue is brought into one band of 4.5 to 6.4, a step lighter than the text, mirroring the dark variant.
Text colorVitesseDark: Vitesse's warm white #dbd7ca, dimmed to #c3c0b4. Light: Vitesse Light's #393a34 unchanged. Variables and properties all use it.
Flat panels, hairline bordersVitesseSidebar, tabs, status bar and editor share one background.
Green accent, cursor, selectionVitesseOne accent everywhere: active tab, badges, buttons, cursor, and a green-tinted selection.
Keywords in greenBothRosé Pine gives keywords its pine color. That slot holds Vitesse's green: lifted to 6.8 contrast in dark, unchanged in light.
Gold strings, rose functions, foam types, iris parametersRosé PineSame hues and roles, at lower brightness and saturation so they stay a step below the text and level with each other. The original values are most of the terminal's bright colors.
Red only for errorsVitesseRosé Pine's pink-red reads as pink on neutral black. Errors use Vitesse's red, and nothing in ordinary code does.
Green-grey italic commentsBothVitesse's comment color, Rosé Pine's italics.
Dimmed string quotes, quiet punctuationVitesseDelimiters recede so the content reads first. VS Code only for the quotes.
Blue for info and modified filesVitesseRosé Pine has no true blue, and a terminal needs one.
